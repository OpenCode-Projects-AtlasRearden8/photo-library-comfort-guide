# Photo Library Comfort Guide

Welcome, friend. If you have ever opened your camera roll, scrolled for ten seconds, felt your stomach drop, and closed it again — you are in exactly the right place. This is not an app-shaming blog post. This is a rescue manual you can pick up once a month, use for about an hour, and put down feeling better.

It is written in plain English, step by step, never condescending. It assumes you have a phone full of photos and exactly zero desire to become a professional photo archivist. You do not need an app, an account, or a subscription to use this guide — everything here works with whatever phone or computer you already have.

## What this guide is

A camera-roll rescue manual built around **one steady ritual**: a one-hour triage session you repeat monthly. The hour is friendly, timed, and bounded, so it can never eat your whole weekend. Every chapter plugs into that rhythm.

## What this is not

This guide will never ask you to:

- Install a subscription you did not already have
- Tag, rate, or caption thousands of old photos by hand
- Feel guilty about the backlog you already have

Your backlog is not a failure. It is a testimony that you had a life worth photographing.

## The chapters, in order

1. **The Monthly Triage Hour** — the exact 60-minute flow you repeat each month. This is the spine of the whole guide.
2. **Duplicates, Decoded** — exact copies versus near-dupes (the blurry ones, the accidental button shots) and what to do about each.
3. **Albums You'll Actually Keep** — a tiny, boring album structure that survives real life.
4. **Naming and Dating Scans** — file names that sort themselves, and how to handle photos with unknown dates.
5. **Cloud or Your Own Drive** — the real trade-off between convenience and control, in honest terms.
6. **Sensitive Photos, Handled Carefully** — IDs, medical moments, and kids' photos, with no judgment and no drama.
7. **Print, Books, and Gifts** — turning the roll you love into a book, a calendar, or a frame that lights up a room.
8. **Retiring an Old Phone Safely** — get the photos off, wipe it clean, and send it off with thanks.

Then two reference pieces you can open any time:

- **Glossary** — every term here, defined like a friend would define it.
- **Monthly Worksheet** — a printable checklist for your triage hour. This is the one page you may want on paper.

## How to use this guide

**Option A — the comfortable pace.** Read "The Monthly Triage Hour" once (ten minutes, with tea). Then do your first session this week using the worksheet. Read one other chapter per week after that. In a month, you will have touched every topic without ever studying.

**Option B — the need-it-now path.** Open the chapter for the problem biting you today, fix that, close the laptop, feel virtuous. The guide is built so every chapter stands on its own.

## A note on energy and focus

Some chapters call out **low-energy paths**, marked like this. If attention, fatigue, ADHD, or chronic illness make long tasks hard, take the low-energy path every time. A gentle system you actually use beats a perfect system you abandon. Nobody is grading you.
