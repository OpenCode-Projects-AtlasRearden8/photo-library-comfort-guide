# Duplicates, Decoded

Duplicates are where the camera roll earns its reputation. Nobody meant to keep six copies of the beach; the beach photo simply arrived six times, and now deleting the beach feels like a memory referendum. Let's dissolve that feeling with categories, because there are really only three kinds of duplicate, and each one has a different job.

## The three kinds of duplicate

| Kind | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact duplicate** | Byte-for-byte copy: shared to yourself twice, downloaded twice, saved twice | Delete one without ceremony |
| **Near-dupe** | Same subject, seconds apart, small differences in focus, pose, or angle | Keep one [hero shot](glossary.md#hero-shot), harsh-review the rest |
| **Accident** | [Pocket shots](glossary.md#pocket-shot), lens-capped blurs, half-pressed buttons, wayward bursts | Delete the same minute you notice it |

Exact duplicates are pure housekeeping. Near-dupes are where judgment lives. Accidents are simply noise, and chapter one's sweep block already clears most of them.

## Finding exact duplicates

Use the tool you already own first:

- **iPhone, iOS 16 and newer:** the Photos app has a built-in *Duplicates* album (search for it inside Albums). It finds exact copies and some near-dupes, and merging keeps the best copy with all your metadata.
- **Android:** Google Photos has a similar cleanup inside Free up space, and many gallery apps include a "find similar" or "clean up" tool.
- **On a computer:** your sibling project [photo-dedupe-lite-cli](glossary.md#photo-dedupe-lite-cli) compares files on your drive and picks safe deletions, written for exactly this chapter.

When you merge or delete an exact duplicate, nothing is lost. You are removing a copy of the photograph, not the photograph. Say that once if the beach-turned-referendum feeling returns.

## Near-dupes: the gentle tie-breaker list

For near-dupes the question is never "which one is prettiest" in a vacuum — it is "which one does future-you need?" When a burst of shots of the same moment competes, pick the winner by these tests in order:

1. **The subject is doing the subject.** Eyes open, mid-laugh, candle blown, crosswalk crossed. Prefer the photo where the moment happens.
2. **Sharpness on the thing you care about.** Zoom to your subject and check edges. Slight whole-image blur loses to a crisp face every time, even if another frame feels more dramatic.
3. **Least clutter.** Background noise, a stranger mid-sneeze, a car crossing the frame — prefer the version where the story is the only thing happening.
4. **Framing.** A little headroom, subject off-center, horizon level if it is a landscape. A slightly imperfect crop can be fixed later; a photo of the moment cannot be retaken.
5. **Exposure.** Prefer the frame where faces are shadows-and-lights sensible rather than ghost-white.

Then one rule holds it all together: **one hero shot, and move on.** Near-dupes you did not star go in the delete pile. If your heart lurches, you may keep exactly one runner-up. That is the whole budget: heroes and understudies, never the full cast.

## Blurry, buttons, and other accidents

Some photos were never going to make it, and recognizing them fast is a skill that saves whole evenings:

- **Motion blur on hands.** If the fingers ghost, the photo records motion, not the person.
- **The button shot.** Shot with a lens pressed against clothing, glass, or a drop of water: the frame is mostly blush-gray with a bright smudge. Delete on sight. They usually arrive in bursts when a phone woke up in a pocket or bag.
- **Focus-hunting shots.** The phone alternated near and far focus while zooming: three nearly identical dark frames of a tablecloth. Keep one if the group is in it; delete the rest.
- **The forgotten screenshot.** Saved a receipt to send to someone, sent it, never deleted it. Screenshots are the largest single species of clutter in most rolls; the sweep block eats them.

A word about bursts: they are usually the most honest friend you have. The burst took 25 photos, two are miracles, and the roll kept all 25. Select the best two, delete the other 23, and let a burst purgatory album hold any burst you cannot judge today, with a rule: anything in purgatory at next month's hour is deleted except the two you starred.

## The deletion-safety caveat

Deleting a photo in one synced place deletes it everywhere — including your "saved" copy in the cloud, if you let the two talk. Chapter [Cloud or Your Own Drive](05-cloud-or-drive.md) covers this in full. If you have not yet set up that two-copies arrangement, do your duplicate deletions **before** installing any new sync, and keep the [two-copies rule](glossary.md#two-copies-rule) in mind for anything you would genuinely cry over.

When in doubt, pause at the delete prompt and finish at next month's hour. A photo kept one more month costs nothing but a little space. A photo deleted in a hurry can hurt for years. Be brisk, but not cruel.
