# Albums You'll Actually Keep

Ask anyone with 40 albums and 6,000 unlabeled photos: folder systems grow until they collapse. The fix is not better labels. The fix is fewer albums, chosen after the questions you actually ask. This chapter gives you a structure that survives forgotten months, a second phone, and the day you feel low on information and energy.

## The retrieval test

An album exists to answer one future question. Before creating one, ask: **"Five years from now, in what precise search would I want this album to hand me the photo back?"** If you cannot phrase it, skip the album — the camera roll's timeline, [search box](glossary.md#metadata), and face grouping already answer a surprising number of these questions without any folder labor.

Modern photo apps find dogs, beaches, weddings, and documents on their own. Albums earn their keep only when search is not enough: when photos belong together *but* are scattered through three different years, like trip photos or a grandchild's visits.

## The starter set: six albums and an archive

Keep the set at six. These cover almost every retrieval that matters:

| Album | Use it for | Example retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Candid moments with the humans you love, across years | "the one of Ana sleeping through church" |
| **Home and Plants** | Projects, upgrades, repairs, your yard's four seasons | "what the hallway looked like before" |
| **Pets and Nature** | Animals, trees, garden, weather, your garden's growth | "what the rose bed looked like in April" |
| **Trips** | Travel, day trips, vacations, hotels | "the harbor ticket, and the secret beach" |
| **Practical and Records** | IDs, receipts, insurance claims, and paperwork you photograph | "the oven serial number" |
| **Archive (cold)** | Anything meaningful but not browsed — old copies, family prints | "grandma's 1970s slide scans" |

Put Trips on your first home screen — it is the album you open at the ticket counter. The Practical and Records album doubles as a life-admin shelf: photograph a page, and it lives in one searchable place forever.

## The low-energy path

On a tired day, sorting into six albums is still a decision machine. Take the two-album method instead — this is the whole system after a hard week:

- **Starred** (your phone's favorites or a single album named for the everyday "best of"): contains only things you love right now
- **Everything else stays in the camera roll**, ordered by date, which is already a system

When energy returns, the monthly triage hour's block three can promote specific starred items into the albums they belong in. A [low-energy path](00-start-here.md#a-note-on-energy-and-focus) is never a failure state; it is a working system.

## How to avoid album sprawl

Album bloat rarely comes from laziness — it comes from well-meaning precision. Prevent it with three habits:

- **Name albums for the retrieval, not the year.** "Honeymoon - 2025" ages fine; "Screen shots - misc 3" rarely does.
- **No sub-threads.** One level of nesting, maximum. A maze inside Photos helps nobody.
- **Audit once a year.** At the January photo hour, delete any album you did not open in two months and whose contents you would not miss. Combine two thin albums; holidays end; borders blur. The archive is safe to absorb them.

If you feel the urge to album *everything*, stop and remember: the camera roll itself is a good system. Scroll from newest, use the year jump at the top of the timeline, and let the line of dates do the sorting. Albums are a spice, not the meal.
