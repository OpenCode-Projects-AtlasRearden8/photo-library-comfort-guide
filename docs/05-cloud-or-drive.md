# Cloud or Your Own Drive

Somewhere in your phone, a photo syncs to a data center tonight. Whether that should be true of *all* of your photos — and where your original copies should live — is the one decision in this guide with real trade-offs. Let's walk it like friends, with the fear taken out and the arithmetic left in.

## The shelf metaphor

**Cloud sync is renting a shelf. It is genuinely convenient: someone else keeps the shelf stocked, mirrors it on every device, and fetches things when you ask.**

**Your own drive is owning the shelf. It lives in your house. Nobody can reprice it, change its terms, or hold it hostage. It does require that you actually take care of a physical object.**

Most comfortable homes use both: a primary copy you own, plus a rented shelf as the convenience layer. That is the [two-copies rule](glossary.md#two-copies-rule), and it is the punchline of this entire chapter.

## The trade-off, in a table

| | Cloud sync | Your own drive |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Mind-numbingly easy | Requires a computer, or a cable, once |
| Daily life | Automatic, everywhere | Automatic after setup, local only |
| Cost | Monthly or yearly, forever | Drive once; a replacement drive now and then |
| If a device dies | Photos come back, fully | Photos come back from your backup copy |
| If you stop paying | Cloud copies may shrink or lock | Nothing changes; you own the files |
| Who can look | The provider and wherever their servers live | Anyone with the drive — usually just you |
| Deleting a photo | Deleted everywhere, instantly | Deleted only where you deleted it |

That last row is the one that surprises people most. Sync means deletions propagate. Deleting a suspect photo at home deletes it in Seattle, then Tokyo, then everywhere it was mirrored — usually within a minute. That is why the [duplicates chapter](02-duplicates-decoded.md#the-deletion-safety-caveat) warns you to finish judgment before you lean on sync.

## The privacy trade, plainly

Storing photos with a provider means they exist on servers you do not control, in places you did not choose. Let's be accurate, not alarmist, about what that actually means:

- **Provider access.** Companies have the technical ability to look at (or process) content in most consumer cloud services; privacy policies, not general human goodness, are what protect you. Some services offer end-to-end encryption for photos; most free tiers do not.
- **Automatic scanning.** To be fair by default: major providers stopped scanning personal photo content for advertising years ago, but they do compute things you agreed to in the terms — thumbnails, search indexes, "memories", and, in some tools, automated content checks when things are uploaded. Policies also change. That change-ability is the privacy trade in one sentence.
- **Jurisdiction.** Data exists in a legal place. Whose law applies there is worth one thoughtful minute, especially for anything [sensitive](06-sensitive-photos.md).
- **Account lockout.** A mis-flagged login, a stolen password, a forgotten verification factor — a cloud-only library is one account problem away from hurt. Your own drive is one *theft or fire* away from hurt, which is why the two-copies rule pairs the risks.

None of this makes cloud wrong. It makes cloud a conscious choice. Convenience is genuinely worth paying for; the mistake is only ever paying for it **unknowingly**.

## The two-copies rule

Any photo worth tears should exist in two independent places on two independent systems, one of them offsite. Practical shapes that work:

| Shape | Setup | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Local primary + cloud mirror** | Copy to your computer or drive; let cloud sync run as the convenience copy | Anyone with a computer, or a family where people share photos |
| **Cloud primary + drive backup** | Cloud autofills; once a quarter, export to a drive that lives somewhere else | Phone-only households with no computer |
| **Two drives, one offsite** | Drive A at home; drive B in a drawer at work, a relative's house, or a safe deposit box | Strongest privacy, lowest recurring cost |

The monthly photo hour makes the quarterly export its export-eve ritual: one specific hour every quarter, with the drive already on the desk. For the general art of copy discipline, schedules, and spotting a failing drive, your sibling guide [backup-basics-handbook](glossary.md#backup-basics-handbook) covers the world in friendly, thorough detail.

## Cloud mistakes worth avoiding

- **Social media is not backup.** Copies there are compressing without asking, and companies remove access for reasons unrelated to your feelings.
- **One cloud, no local copy, forever.** Fine while you pay. Ruthless the day a card expires while you're abroad — or when a term changes.
- **Pocket drive as the only copy.** A single small drive is a single small fire. Two drives, one elsewhere: real.
- **Syncing a sensitive [hidden album](glossary.md#hidden-album) with no thought.** It syncs — to every device that shares the account, including the family tablet. Read [Sensitive Photos, Handled Carefully](06-sensitive-photos.md) before enabling anything.

## Do the annual shelf check

Once a year, at your January session, spend ten minutes asking: can I actually restore a photo right now, from each copy, on purpose? Pick a random old photo, find it, open it, explain to a pretend grandchild when and where it was made. That exercise is the whole audit. If either copy fails it, fix the copy — the drive itself is telling you something safer learned now rather than later.
