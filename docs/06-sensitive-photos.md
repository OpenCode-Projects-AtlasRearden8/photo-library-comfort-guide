# Sensitive Photos, Handled Carefully

Some photos deserve different treatment. Not silence, not shame — just the extra inch of care you already give anything fragile. This chapter is that inch, applied calmly: what counts as sensitive, how to store and share it, what to do when sharing goes sideways, and how to sweep once a quarter so the care stays fresh.

One honest caveat first: this guide is general education about photos and privacy. It is never legal, financial, or medical advice — if a decision has real stakes, this chapter helps you think; it does not decide for you.

## What counts as sensitive

Nothing on this list is scandalous. These are simply photos where **the content is fine for you but harmful in the wrong hands**:

- **Identity and money documents.** Passports, driver's licenses, insurance cards, tax or claim paperwork you photographed for convenience, credit cards (front *and* back — people forget the backs).
- **Medical moments.** Wound photos, test results, medication lists, portal screenshots. For the nurses, techs, and therapists reading this: this chapter uses your habits as the model for everyone, and it is education only — your organization's own rules always outrank a photo guide.
- **Kids.** Children's faces, school uniforms with a school name visible, birthday-party guest photos, the walk-home pictures that reveal a daily route.
- **Home clues.** Package labels showing your address, the front door with a house number, license plates of the car parked inside the frame — small fragments that assemble into a location over time.
- **Grief and intimacy.** Hospital vigils, funerals, hard conversations, private photos with a partner. Not embarrassing; simply not everyone's business.

The [Practical and Records album](03-albums-youll-actually-keep.md) is exactly the right home for paperwork photographs — and the first place to look during a sensitive sweep.

## Storing them well, on your own terms

Your phone already ships with the tools; use what fits your comfort level:

| Level | How | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Hidden, not locked** | Your phone's basic hidden album | Out of the main roll, still visible in settings |
| **Locked** | Hidden album behind Face ID or fingerprint (built into iOS 17-plus; Android's Secure Folder does the same) | Unlocks with *you* |
| **Vault** | A photo-vault app, or encrypted documents put in a drive you control | Off the roll entirely |

Two habits make all three work:

- **Photographs of documents are conveniences, not vaults.** The original passport, card, or letter is the thing; the photo is a copy you keep so a locked-out day hurts less. Delete document photos the day the need is over ("delete after the mortgage closes").
- **Neutral names over revealing names.** `scan-2026-03.jpg` is safer than `passport-front.jpg` when you are emailing, sharing, or backing up. Names travel; the photo's permission does not.

## The think-before-upload test

Before a sensitive photo syncs anywhere, it has to pass one honest question: **"If this synced to every device I share the account with, would I be okay? And if a human at the provider saw it during a bug report, would I be okay?"**

That question, not paranoia, is the whole test. Plenty of answers are comfortable: a garden in spring, a long-awaited doctor's visit, a family beach day — ordinary joy at either question. Others are not: a tax letter, an intimate moment, a hospital room during the hardest week of someone's life. Motivation here is choice, not fear: you are choosing where copies of your life live. For the wider mechanics of where those copies end up, that is [Cloud or Your Own Drive](05-cloud-or-drive.md)'s job.

## Sharing: expiry, backs, and mismatched plans

- **Prefer expiry links.** Photo-sharing links with expiration (both big ecosystems offer temporary album links) are designed to disappear. Attachments in email do not expire, and they outlive conversations for months.
- **Zip and lock documents.** When an ID simply must travel, put the photo in a document folder, and ask the recipient how they prefer to receive it. Most banks and agencies tell you precisely, and it is rarely "just attach it."
- **Kids and group chats.** Before posting a child's face anywhere with an audience, glance at three things: a school name in frame, other children whose parents did not approve, and location tags. A 10-second habit, never a panic.
- **Ask before sharing grief.** Of every sensitive category, this is the one with the simplest rule: ask first, share second. People in hard weeks remember who curbed their photo.

## If a sensitive photo goes somewhere it shouldn't

Breathe first. Then, in this order:

1. **Delete the copy where it traveled** — the message, the post, the wrong album.
2. **Change anything a leak actually exposes.** If the photo shows a card or ID, call the card or agency after the delete and ask precisely what they recommend; it is a routine call for them.
3. **Check sync outcomes.** Search every device that shares the account for the photo; sync deletes are fast but reminders are free.
4. **For a man-made share**, tell one trusted person what happened so your brain keeps one chapter, not eleven rewound loops.

Then put the phone down. If the error was a typo of stability, correction is minutes. If a person deliberately spread it, that is a real problem with real support channels — and none of the four steps above lose their value because a crime also occurred.

## The quarterly sensitive sweep

Add one block to every third monthly session — it is four minutes of outsized peace:

- [Open the Practical and Records album](03-albums-youll-actually-keep.md) and delete any document photos whose jobs are done
- Check all your [hidden](glossary.md#hidden-album) and locked storage: still unlocked by the right face, still nothing in it that no longer needs locking
- Skim recent months for address labels, IDs, or children's names in frames that snuck into the main roll
- Ask one question out loud: "Is there anything in this library that belongs in a vault instead of a roll?"

The sweep is a kindness to a future you who will never need to remember. That is what this chapter is for.
