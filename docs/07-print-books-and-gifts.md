# Print, Books, and Gifts

A camera roll is a museum with no visitors. Printing changes that — and it is more achievable than it looks, because the trick is not choosing the perfect 40 photos. The trick is choosing *one small theme* and stopping. This chapter covers photo books, calendars, single prints and frames, and the rare, gorgeous gift of a digital frame for someone else's wall.

## Books: the theme-first method

The reason photo books don't get made is the same every time: people open a blank layout, scroll all 12,000 photos, and drown. The theme-first method turns the whole thing upside down so that you pick from a pile of 25, not a pile of 12,000:

1. **Pick the theme.** One trip, one season, one year, one baby, one house project finished. Speaking plainly: "our spring" is a theme; "our everything" is not.
2. **Build the pile at the photo hour.** Star 25–35 photos into a temporary album named for the book. This takes one favorites block, not a whole weekend.
3. **Pre-write the captions.** On paper or in your notes app first: 15 words each, **names plus the year** ("Lake Michigan, 2024 — Ana's first ferry"). Caption-when-facing-the-layout stalls the book; caption-from-a-list finishes it.
4. **Use assembled layouts.** Every major service ships templates that look designed if you simply fill them in order. Fill, glance, ship.
5. **Order the proof.** One copy, for you. Love it? Order copies for others at holiday time, when services run deep-discount, and you will save real money.

A book needs no perfection to matter. It needs names on faces — including yours, in photos you are usually the one taking.

## Resolution, in one gentle table

When picking a photo for print, zoom in first. If it looks punchy while magnified to print size, it will print with punch:

| Print size | What works comfortably | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| 4×6 inches | Almost any modern phone photo | Piles, albums, gifts |
| 8×10 inches | Phone photos taken within the last decade, kept uncropped | Frames, desks, walls |
| Poster sizes or full-page book spreads | Serious camera shots, or top-tier scene phones | Statement walls, big books |

The zoom check beats the arithmetic every time, because faces matter more than file specs. A slightly softer 8×10 of your laughing father beats a technically flawless print of an empty landscape in every frame that will ever hang — including [hero shots](glossary.md#hero-shot) you had nearly deleted.

## Calendars

Calendars are the friendliest beginner print: twelve photos, one per month, filled with things you already know how to pick. Take them from your favorites block — twelve is under a month of favorites for most people. Two practical notes: **order in early November** for December gifting, because print pricing and delivery lines are kindest when you are early, and stagger the twelve photos across seasons, so the calendar gives someone a whole year of the family rather than one luminous summer.

## Single prints and the gift frame

One photo, chosen with love, outperforms a whole gallery wall almost every time:

- **The desk print.** Your own favorite, 8×10, simple frame. It belongs where you work, not where your guests judge.
- **The candid-first rule for gifts.** For a partner, parent, or close friend, prefer the laughing-talking photo over the posed portrait. People keep candid frames and retire perfect ones.
- **Print twice.** Print your gift copy *and* one archival copy for your own albums. Gifts that travel live long lives away from home; the original deserves a home too.

## Digital frames, with care

A digital frame is a small screen, quietly cycling through a photo feed — the gift that keeps a faraway grandparent inside the family day. The care comes from choosing what feeds it:

- Prefer a frame that pulls from a private, invite-only album that *you* curate, rather than one that asks the recipient's relatives to upload willy-nilly into a public-ish stream.
- Load it at the photo hour from the favorites pile, so what cycles past is the year's stars, not every sunrise.
- When the gift is for someone who shares your [sensitive-photos](06-sensitive-photos.md) concerns, keep the feed to the people-and-trips albums; keep the paperwork moments off the shelf.

Set it up *before* you wrap it — the single best gift upgrade is walking into the room with the frame already showing a favorite face.

## Budget tiers, no guilt

Nice things exist at every price, which means the budget is never the obstacle:

- **Small:** one 8×10 and a basic frame, any month
- **Medium:** an annual calendar for the family, or one theme-first book per year
- **Large:** a coffee-table book of a decade, split into quarterly piles so the making never bloats

And beneath every tier sits the same safety net: your [two-copies rule](glossary.md#two-copies-rule) originals, untouched. Prints and books are love letters *about* the library. The library itself stays exactly where chapter five put it.
