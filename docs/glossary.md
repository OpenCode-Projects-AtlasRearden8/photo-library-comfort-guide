# Glossary

Every term this guide leans on, defined the way a friend would: plainly, with the why attached. Terms are alphabetical — use your browser's find-in-page, or jump from any chapter link that brought you here.

### Album

A named collection of photos inside your gallery app. An album does not copy photos; it just points at them. That is why albums are cheap to make — and why [Albums You'll Actually Keep](03-albums-youll-actually-keep.md) asks for so few of them.

### Archive

The long shelf: photos you keep for the record, not for browsing. In this guide, the Archive album holds anything meaningful that you rarely open, plus old prints and scans. Cold and happy.

### Backup

A second, independent copy of your library that survives when the first copy is lost, broken, or stolen. A backup is only real if it lives on a different device than the original — a copy sitting on the same phone is just a dupe with good intentions.

### backup-basics-handbook

Your sibling guide from the same family as this one: a friendly, thorough walkthrough of copies, schedules, and drive health. When the [two-copies rule](#two-copies-rule) raises questions, that handbook answers them in depth.

### Burst mode

Your phone's rapid-fire shooting mode, which can take dozens of photos in seconds. Bursts are honest: two or three frames are miracles and the rest are clutter. Choose the winners, delete the cast, monthly.

### Cloud sync

A service that mirrors your photos to company servers and to every device on your account, automatically. Wonderful convenience, real privacy trade — the whole trade is weighed in [Cloud or Your Own Drive](05-cloud-or-drive.md).

### Cold storage

Copies that live off to the side, rarely touched: an external drive in a drawer, a box of labeled archive disks, or a bank vault. Cold storage exists for the worst day, not for Tuesday.

### Exact duplicate

A byte-for-byte copy of a photo — shared to yourself twice, downloaded twice, saved twice. Deleting one copy loses nothing. See [Duplicates, Decoded](02-duplicates-decoded.md).

### EXIF

The small metadata block every camera attaches to a photo: timestamp, camera model, sometimes location. It is why your app can sort by date without your help — and why scans you create yourself have none until you set it.

### Factory reset

The deep wipe that returns a phone to blank-new state: photos, accounts, messages, and passwords all erased. The core step of [Retiring an Old Phone Safely](08-retire-old-phones.md), done only after the photo rescue.

### Favorites

Your phone's star system. A one-tap way to promote photos you love without building anything. Favorites feed every print, book, and calendar in [Print, Books, and Gifts](07-print-books-and-gifts.md).

### Hero shot

The single winning photo from a set of near-dupes — the one future-you needs. One hero per moment; one runner-up allowed when your heart lurches.

### Hidden album

The built-in out-of-sight album your phone already has. It removes photos from the main roll without deleting them. Useful, but remember: hidden is not locked, and hidden albums still [sync](#cloud-sync) — see [Sensitive Photos, Handled Carefully](06-sensitive-photos.md).

### Metadata

Everything a photo carries besides its pixels: date, camera, location, description. Scans arrive without it, which is why [Naming and Dating Scans](04-naming-and-dating-scans.md) spends so much time on dates you set on purpose.

### Near-dupe

Two photos of the same subject, seconds apart, with small differences in focus, pose, or angle. Near-dupes are judgment calls, and the tie-breaker list lives in the duplicates chapter.

### Offsite

Stored somewhere else — a relative's house, an office drawer, a safe deposit box. Offsite is the ingredient that makes a copy survive the same flood, fire, or theft that claims the first.

### photo-dedupe-lite-cli

Your sibling command-line helper from the same family as this guide: it compares files on your computer and proposes safe deletions for the exact-duplicate work in [Duplicates, Decoded](02-duplicates-decoded.md). For people who like a terminal; the phone's built-in tools handle the rest.

### Pocket shot

The accidental photo your phone took from inside a pocket, bag, or hand: ceiling, shoe, steering wheel, or a mysterious blur with a light streak. Delete on sight — it records nothing but the fact that your phone was awake.

### Scan

A digital copy made from paper: prints, slides, negatives, documents. A scan inherits no date and no place, which makes naming and dating it the most valuable ten seconds of the whole project.

### Secure folder

Your phone's locked, encrypted area for photos and files (Apple's locked hidden album, Samsung's Secure Folder, and similar). Unlocks with your face, finger, or passcode. The mid-level home for [sensitive photos](06-sensitive-photos.md).

### Triage

Fast, kind sorting: most important decisions first, obvious cases decided in seconds, everything else deferred without guilt. It is how emergency rooms work, and it is how your monthly photo hour works too.

### Two-copies rule

Any photo worth tears exists in two independent places, on two independent systems, one of them offsite. Local primary plus cloud mirror, cloud primary plus quarterly drive export, or two drives — the shapes live in [Cloud or Your Own Drive](05-cloud-or-drive.md#the-two-copies-rule).
