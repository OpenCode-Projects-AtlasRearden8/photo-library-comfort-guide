# Monthly Triage Worksheet

The one page you keep on the fridge, in the wallet, or under the junk-drawer junk. Print two. Bring one to the photo hour; leave the other untouched as the spare, because spare worksheets are how rituals survive busy months.

**Month:** ______________________  **Date done:** ______________________

## Before the hour

- [ ] Phone charged above 50 percent
- [ ] Do Not Disturb on for one hour
- [ ] Playlist picked, drink poured
- [ ] Old worksheet's "owed" list in front of me

## The hour

**Block 1 — The sweep, minutes 0-10**

- [ ] Screenshots older than 30 days deleted
- [ ] Pocket shots and button shots deleted
- [ ] Blurry accidents deleted
- [ ] Rule respected: hesitate longer than 2 seconds means keep

**Block 2 — Duplicates, minutes 10-30**

- [ ] Built-in duplicate tool run (or photo-dedupe-lite-cli on the computer)
- [ ] Exact duplicates merged or deleted
- [ ] Near-dupes judged with the tie-breaker list, one hero shot per moment
- [ ] Burst leftovers: two winners kept, cast deleted

**Block 3 — Albums, minutes 30-45**

- [ ] This month's keepers placed in the six-album set (or the two-album low-energy path)
- [ ] Nothing filed that took longer than three seconds to place

**Block 4 — Favorites, minutes 45-55**

- [ ] Anything that made me smile is starred
- [ ] One print or gift candidate named

**Block 5 — Close-out, minutes 55-60**

- [ ] Two-line note written (see tally below)
- [ ] Anything owed is written down, not worried about
- [ ] Next month's photo hour exists on the calendar

## Every third month, add these four minutes

- [ ] Sensitive sweep done (document photos whose jobs ended; locked storage skimmed)
- [ ] Export to the drive done (or cloud re-verified, per the two-copies rule)
- [ ] One random old photo restored on purpose, from each copy
- [ ] Album audit: anything unopened in two months merged into Archive

## The rules of the worksheet

1. A skipped month costs nothing. Do a 15-minute sweep and resume.
2. Hesitation is information: it means keep.
3. Two lines of notes beat two hours of scrolling.
4. The goal is a roll you love, not a roll you fear.
